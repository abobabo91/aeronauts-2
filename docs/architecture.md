# Architecture

## Non-negotiable game rules

- The flight body and velocity point in the same direction whenever speed is above a small threshold.
- At zero speed, the body may rotate in place; movement resumes along the body's forward direction.
- Aim View keeps camera and weapon direction independent from the flight body, with a centered crosshair and tunable body-relative angular limits. Forward View aligns camera, crosshair, and weapons with body forward. Switching back to Aim View begins from current forward.
- Movement supports pitch, yaw, roll, full stop/hover, airbrake, boost, spins, and high-G hook turns.
- Pitch is the strongest turning axis, making roll-to-bank plus pitch more efficient than flat yaw. Harder steering bleeds more momentum.
- Airbrake disables thrust and boost while held. Reversals emerge from airbrake, steering, and boost-exit timing; there is no scripted 180-degree maneuver.
- There is no grapple or wall-grab mechanic.
- The primary weapon is a precise, zero-spread finite-speed projectile; travel time creates a leading skill without random inaccuracy.
- The secondary is an instantaneous hitscan railgun. It is stronger, has a limited reserve, and is fired with right mouse/left trigger by default.
- The camera selects the crosshair target, but every authoritative shot originates at the shared head/cockpit region. A player cannot damage through cover that hides this critical region from the firing line.
- Shields are a non-regenerating damage gate: while any shield remains, hit location does not change damage and a shield-breaking hit does not spill into hull. After shield depletion, hit regions determine hull damage. A railgun cockpit hit is the explicit exception and bypasses shield for a lethal hull hit.
- Character-wing and small-aircraft presentations must share a comparable central silhouette and hit-volume layout.

## Runtime boundaries for later implementation

Keep the simulation independent from presentation so the browser demo, native build, replays, bots, and eventual server authority use the same rules.

1. **Input** converts devices into a device-neutral movement intent and aim intent.
2. **Core simulation** advances a fixed 60 Hz tick and owns authoritative time/state.
3. **Flight** turns movement intent into body orientation, speed, boost, airbrake, and maneuver state.
4. **Combat** resolves aim rays, primary/secondary resources, hit regions, shields, and damage.
5. **Camera** follows aim intent and reads simulation state without controlling velocity.
6. **Presentation** interpolates visuals, animation, audio, trails, and effects from simulation state.
7. **UI** reads public state and emits menu/loadout commands; it does not mutate flight or combat directly.
8. **Networking** will later serialize input/state at these boundaries rather than networking scene objects ad hoc.

Use data assets for tunable flight, weapon, shield, camera, and input curves. Avoid burying balance constants in scene objects. Keep gameplay state out of animation and visual-effect components.

## Asset ownership

`Assets/Aeronauts2` is the first-party namespace:

- `Art/Characters`: pilots, bodies, rigs, and character presentation
- `Art/Vehicles`: aircraft-form presentation sharing the common gameplay frame
- `Art/Environments`: arena geometry and background set dressing
- `Art/Materials` and `Art/VFX`: rendering assets
- `Audio/Music` and `Audio/SFX`: authored audio
- `Input`: action maps and device-specific tuning
- `Prefabs`: composed gameplay and presentation prefabs by domain
- `Scenes/Bootstrap`: startup and persistent systems
- `Scenes/FlightLab`: isolated movement/combat experimentation
- `Scenes/Tests`: integration test scenes only
- `Settings`: game-owned configuration assets
- `Scripts`: future runtime modules matching the boundaries above
- `Tests`: future EditMode and PlayMode tests
- `UI`: menus, HUD, reticles, and accessibility presentation
- `Editor`: project-only tools such as the reproducible Flight Lab scene generator

Third-party content stays in `Assets/ThirdParty/<publisher-or-package>` and must include its license/provenance. Do not edit third-party sources in place; wrap or derive them under `Assets/Aeronauts2`.

## Platform strategy

The first build target is WebGL for easy sharing. The native target is Windows IL2CPP for a later Steam release. Keep core input, simulation, combat, and presentation portable across both.

Browser constraints to preserve from the start:

- no dependency on native plugins in the core game;
- avoid large startup downloads and excessive texture/audio memory;
- treat browser pause, focus loss, and controller reconnection as normal;
- networking must use a browser-compatible transport such as secure WebSockets or WebRTC;
- quality tiers must allow cheaper shadows, post-processing, particles, and render scale.

The browser build is generated by the editor-only `Aeronauts2.Editor.Build.BuildPipeline.BuildWebGL` method into ignored `Build/WebGL` output. `tools/serve_webgl.py` supplies required Brotli and WASM headers, while `tools/browser_smoke.py` attaches Playwright to an isolated CDP Chrome and verifies the rendered canvas, input, combat, console/network health, screenshots, and frame timing. `BrowserTelemetryReporter` and `Plugins/WebGL/Aeronauts2Browser.jslib` expose a read-only, WebGL-only state snapshot so automation can assert movement speed and weapon outcomes without changing authoritative gameplay. This is a repeatable local browser gate, not a deployment host.

The first online architecture decision happens only after the offline flight lab feels good. At that checkpoint, evaluate server-authoritative prediction/reconciliation and browser-compatible relay hosting. The combat design should assume lag compensation will be required for precise hitscan.

## Implemented Flight Lab boundary

The first vertical slice uses one `Aeronauts2.Runtime` assembly and one EditMode test assembly. Folders and namespaces preserve the module boundaries without paying for a separate assembly per tiny prototype module. Split assemblies only when a real dependency or build boundary requires it.

`FlightSimulation.Step` is the 60 Hz authoritative movement operation. It owns position, orientation, scalar speed, velocity, boost energy, boost lockout, airbrake timing, perfect-exit state, and automatic-roll progress. Velocity is derived from body forward and scalar speed every tick, enforcing alignment by construction. At zero speed velocity is exactly zero while rotation remains available.

Airbrake turn authority scales with current speed. A hook turn therefore emerges from steering while airbraking, followed by acceleration/boost, rather than from a dedicated canned reverse action. A perfect boost exit requires at least `0.18 s` of airbrake entered at speed `18` or higher, followed by a fresh boost press inside a `0.15 s` release window. Its extra acceleration lasts `0.22 s`; these are initial playtest values in `FlightTuning.asset`, not fixed design promises.

`MovementIntent` carries continuous device-neutral steering, roll, throttle, brake, and boost state. `ManeuverIntent` carries one-tick commands such as an automatic roll. `RollGestureDetector` recognizes same-side double taps from separate left/right actions; `InputTuning.asset` owns the initial `0.25 s` double-tap window. The 360-degree roll is simulated over `0.4 s`, suppresses manual roll for its duration, and still permits pitch/yaw input so players can shape a corkscrew. It provides no invulnerability.

`AimConstraint` clamps world aim against yaw/up/down limits in the body's rolled local frame without making in-bounds aim follow body rotation. `AimTuning.asset` owns two editable profiles: constrained `H110/U78/D78` and full-360 `H180/U89/D89`; `V`/Select switches them for Flight Lab comparison. `C`/right-stick press toggles gameplay `Aim` and `Forward` camera states. Forward View ignores aim delta and derives the camera/weapon ray from body forward every frame; returning to Aim View initializes independent aim from that direction. Flight reset emits an event so the camera recenters atomically with the reset body.

`FireIntent` carries continuous primary state and a queued secondary-press command. `WeaponSimulation.Step` owns fixed-tick fire cadence, railgun cooldown, and reserve state independently from presentation. `PlayerWeaponController` uses a two-stage query: the centered camera ray chooses the intended point, then a second ray begins at `HeadCockpitCombatOrigin` and establishes the actual shot direction and obstruction state. Primary bolts spawn there and sphere-cast as they advance; the railgun ray and trace start there in the firing tick. Player-owned colliders are ignored, but world cover is not. A blocked shot hits cover and consumes its normal projectile/cooldown/reserve rather than being cancelled. Both weapons stop on the closest collider; the prototype railgun does not currently pierce targets.

Initial `WeaponTuning.asset` values are `120` units/second, `0.16` radius, `3 s` lifetime, and `8` shots/second for the primary; the railgun has `500` range, `0.85 s` cooldown, and three shots. Explicit damage profiles replace a global critical multiplier. The primary deals `16` to shield or body hull and `100` to an unshielded cockpit. The railgun deals `80` to shield or body hull and `100` to a cockpit, with its cockpit profile marked to bypass shield. These remain playtest values rather than settled balance.

`TargetState` and `TargetSimulation` own the fixed-tick damage lifecycle independently from presentation. `TargetTuning.asset` currently supplies `300` non-regenerating shield, `100` hull, and a `3 s` respawn. `WeaponDamageProfile` declares separate shield, body-hull, and critical-hull damage plus the exceptional critical shield-bypass flag. Damage reports explicitly separate shield and hull damage and identify shield-break, shield-bypass, critical, and elimination transitions. `CombatantHealth` is the shared runtime presentation adapter for player, bot, and drill targets; `TargetDummy` is only a thin training-target specialization, `TargetHitRegion` classifies body versus cockpit hits, and `TargetMover` supplies fixed-tick training motion.

The generated player and bot have the same central body/wing/cockpit hit-volume layout and both fire from `HeadCockpitCombatOrigin`. `PlayerCombatantController` disables player flight/fire while eliminated and restores them on respawn. `BotFlightController` creates device-neutral intent and advances the same `FlightSimulation.Step` used by the player, so its velocity/body alignment and flight tuning are not separate approximations. `BotWeaponController` currently uses the shared primary cadence, speed, collision, and damage rules with independently tunable leading, aim error, reaction, range, and burst behavior in `BotTuning.asset`; the bot does not use the limited railgun in this validation milestone. `DuelMatchController` accepts only eliminations against the opposing combatant and freezes a first-to-five result until manual reset. `CombatDrillController` coordinates manual reset across both combatants, drill targets, weapon state, and diagnostics. The HUD only reads public combat/match state.

Unity physics queries are acceptable for the offline lab; before networking, collision queries must move behind a deterministic/server-authoritative combat boundary rather than networking these scene components directly. `TargetState` is deliberately plain simulation state so it can later be owned by a server, replay, or bot without importing HUD or renderer behavior.
